Application of Derivatives

Maxima & Minima Optimization Problems

Optimization involves finding the maximum or minimum value of a function. Derivatives give us the exact rate of change—setting f'(x) = 0 reveals the critical points where extreme values occur.

Interactive 3-Sided Garden Optimizer

Adjust total fence length to maximize the enclosed area against a straight wall.

60m

Mathematical Derivation

1. Setup Constraint: 2x + y = 60 ⇒ y = 60 - 2x

2. Area Function: A(x) = x · y = x(60 - 2x) = 60x - 2x²

3. Take Derivative: A'(x) = 60 - 4x

4. Critical Point (A'(x)=0): 4x = 60 ⇒ x = 15 m

5. Second Derivative Test: A''(x) = -4 < 0 ⇒ Confirmed Maximum!

Optimal Width (x) 15 m
Optimal Length (y) 30 m
Max Area (A) 450 m²
1

Find Critical Points

Take the first derivative f'(x), set it to 0, and solve for x. These identify flat stationary points where the graph turns.

2

1st Derivative Test

Check the sign of f'(x) on either side of critical point c:

  • (+) to (-) : Local Maximum
  • (-) to (+) : Local Minimum
3

2nd Derivative Test

Evaluate f''(c) at critical point c:

  • f''(c) < 0 : Concave down ⇒ Maximum
  • f''(c) > 0 : Concave up ⇒ Minimum
Fundamental Rules

Rules for Finding Derivatives

Master the foundational rules of differentiation: Power Rule, Product Rule, Quotient Rule, and the Chain Rule for composite functions.

Power Rule

Basic
d/dx [ xn ] = n · xn-1

Bring the exponent to the front and decrease the power by 1.

Example: d/dx [ 5x&sup4; ] = 5 · (4x³) = 20x³

Product Rule

Combination
d/dx [ u · v ] = u · v' + v · u'

"First times derivative of second plus second times derivative of first."

Example: d/dx [ x² · ex ] = x²ex + ex(2x) = xex(x + 2)

Quotient Rule

Fractions
d/dx [ u / v ] = (v · u' - u · v') / v²

"Low d-high minus high d-low, over the square of what's below."

Example: d/dx [ x / (x+1) ] = [ (x+1)(1) - x(1) ] / (x+1)² = 1 / (x+1)²

Chain Rule

Composite
d/dx [ f(g(x)) ] = f'(g(x)) · g'(x)

Derivative of outside function evaluated inside, multiplied by derivative of inside.

Example: d/dx [ (3x+1)&sup5; ] = 5(3x+1)&sup4; · (3) = 15(3x+1)&sup4;

Sine

d/dx [ sin(x) ] = cos(x)

Cosine

d/dx [ cos(x) ] = -sin(x)

Tangent

d/dx [ tan(x) ] = sec²(x)

Cosecant

d/dx [ csc(x) ] = -csc(x)cot(x)

Secant

d/dx [ sec(x) ] = sec(x)tan(x)

Cotangent

d/dx [ cot(x) ] = -csc²(x)
Advanced Derivatives

Higher-Order & Implicit Differentiation

Learn to compute second, third, and n-th derivatives, as well as finding dy/dx for equations where y cannot be explicitly isolated.

Higher-Order Derivatives

Taking the derivative of a derivative yields higher-order functions. For motion: Position s(t) → Velocity v(t) = s'(t) → Acceleration a(t) = s''(t).

1st Derivative: f'(x) = dy/dx
2nd Derivative: f''(x) = d²y/dx²
3rd Derivative: f'''(x) = d³y/dx³
Worked Example:

Find f''(x) for f(x) = x&sup4; - 3x² + 5

1. f'(x) = 4x³ - 6x

2. f''(x) = 12x² - 6

Implicit Differentiation

When x and y are intermingled (e.g., x² + y² = 25), differentiate both sides with respect to x, applying the Chain Rule to y terms (multiply by dy/dx).

Worked Circle Example:

Find dy/dx for x² + y² = 25

1. Differentiate w.r.t x: 2x + 2y(dy/dx) = 0

2. Solve for dy/dx: 2y(dy/dx) = -2x

3. dy/dx = -2x / 2y = -x / y
